Let your days come to you.

iPhone camera roll · no iCloud needed

An iPhone Shortcut can send daily photos directly to your Mac. Both devices must be on your trusted home Wi-Fi, with the Mac awake and PhotoJournal running. The journal itself stays accessible only on this Mac.

Keep the key private. Photo transfers use HTTP on your local Wi-Fi; enable this only on a trusted home network. Do not forward port 3002 on your router.

Build the iPhone Shortcut
  1. On your iPhone, open Shortcuts, create a shortcut named “Daily PhotoJournal,” and add Find Photos. Filter Date Taken to in the last 2 days and Media Type to Image. Turn Limit off.
  2. Add Repeat with Each photo. Inside the repeat, get Date Taken from the Repeat Item, then Format Date with Custom format yyyy-MM-dd.
  3. Still inside the repeat, add Convert Image: convert the original Repeat Item to JPEG. Select the Repeat Item explicitly, rather than the formatted date.
  4. Add Get Contents of URL with the upload address above. Choose POST, Request Body File, and select the Converted Image.
  5. Add headers: Authorization = Bearer followed by your private upload key; X-Photo-Date = the Formatted Date variable; Content-Type = image/jpeg.
  6. Test with one photo first. Allow photo and local-network access when prompted. If macOS asks, allow incoming connections for Python.
  7. Once it works, add a Time of Day automation, choose this shortcut, and select Run Immediately if offered. Choose a time when your Mac is normally awake. iOS action labels and background permissions vary; test an actual scheduled run.

The last-two-days window catches a missed run, and repeated copies of the same image on the same date are skipped. Uploads missed for longer need a wider date filter and a manual rerun. A changing Mac Wi-Fi address requires updating the Shortcut URL.

Apple Photos on this Mac

Automatically check the last seven days every 10 minutes while PhotoJournal is running. New photos become daily draft pages. Previously imported Apple photos are skipped.

Your iPhone pictures need to appear in the Mac Photos app first, through iCloud Photos or a cable import. macOS will ask permission to control Photos. Only photo copies are imported; your library stays unchanged.

Google Photos

Google’s photo picker requires you to select photos; signing in does not allow this app to automatically read your whole library. For scheduled daily imports from your iPhone, use the Shortcut connection above. Bulk Google Takeout imports are a separate feature still to come.
